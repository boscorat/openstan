# Multi-Account Tracking

Most people have more than one bank account — a current account, a
savings account, maybe a credit card or two. openstan lets you
import statements from all of them and see your complete financial
picture in one place.

[Get Started](../installation.md){ .md-button .md-button--primary }
[Quick Start Guide](../quickstart.md){ .md-button }

---

## The Problem

### Fragmented Financial View

Your money is spread across multiple accounts. To understand your
overall position, you'd need to manually combine data from several
PDF downloads — and keep doing it every month.

### Different Banks, Different Formats

Each bank produces PDF statements in its own layout. There's no
standard format, which makes manual consolidation tedious.

### Privacy Concerns

Most "track your spending" apps require connecting to your bank
via Open Banking or uploading your data to the cloud. If you'd
prefer to keep your financial data on your own machine, the options
are limited.

---

## How openstan Helps

### One Project, Many Accounts

Create a single openstan project and import PDF statements from
every account you hold — current accounts, savings accounts, credit
cards. Each statement is tagged with its source account.

### Searchable Across All Accounts

Search for any transaction across all your accounts simultaneously.
Looking for a specific payment? It doesn't matter which account it
went through — openstan finds it.

### Export a Combined View

Export all transactions as a single spreadsheet, or separate by
account. Filter by date range, amount, or description to see
exactly what you need.

### 100% Offline

No cloud upload. No Open Banking connection. No account to create.
You download your statement PDFs from your bank's website, import
them into openstan, and everything stays on your machine.

## How It Works

1. **[Install openstan](../installation.md)** — native installer, no Python required
2. **Create a project** — [Quick Start guide](../quickstart.md)
3. **Download statement PDFs** from each of your bank accounts
4. **Import them all** — one file at a time or entire folders
5. **Search and filter** — across all accounts at once
6. **Export to Excel/CSV** — [Export guide](../screens/export-data.md)
